Some events include **programming challenges**, solved by submitting a
program rather than a flag — the ICPC-style problem: read standard input,
print the answer to standard output. They carry a **Code** badge on the board
and open on their own page.

## The problem page

The statement sits beside its **Examples** — each an input and the expected
output. The info line gives the **time limit** and **memory limit**; the time
limit depends on the **Language** you pick, since slower languages get a
proportionally longer allowance. **Files** holds anything the authors
attached.

## Writing and submitting

**Your solution** offers two ways in: **Upload a file** (the default — drop
the file you saved in your own editor; the language is detected from its
extension) or **Write code** in the browser, where your draft is kept per
problem and language on this device.

Then **Run examples** to try your program on the visible examples — never
scored — or **Submit** to have it judged against every test, hidden ones
included. Code files are limited to 128 KB, and you can have at most three
submissions waiting at once.

## Verdicts

Results update live: *Waiting to be judged — 3 in line*, then *Judging…*,
then the verdict.

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Accepted** | Passed every test — it scores like a solved flag. |
| **Wrong answer** | Output didn't match on some test. |
| **Time / Memory / Output limit exceeded** | Your program used more than the limit allows. |
| **Runtime error** | It crashed or exited with an error. |
| **Compilation error** | It didn't compile — see **Compiler messages**. |
| **Couldn't be judged** | A problem on the platform's side, not yours — never scored; submit again. |

A failing example shows its input, the expected output and yours; a failing
**hidden test** is named only by its number — its data stays hidden. **Your
submissions** lists past runs and submissions, and **Load code** brings one
back into the editor.

!!! note "Verdicts can change"
    Judges can **rejudge** a problem (after fixing a test, say) or decide a
    verdict by hand. The newest verdict counts, so points can be gained or
    taken back later; such entries are marked *Rejudged* or *Judge's
    decision*.

## Asking the judges

Statement unclear? **Ask the judges** sends a private question — one
message, answered in **Support** (tagged *Question*; to follow up, ask a new
one). If the answer helps everyone, they may share it as a
**clarification**, without your name: it appears on the problem under
*Clarifications from the judges* and arrives as a notification. For flag
challenges, ask through a normal support ticket instead (chapter 7).
