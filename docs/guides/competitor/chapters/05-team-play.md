In a team-mode competition, your team is the scoring unit: solves, points,
hint costs, and guess caps are shared. This chapter is about working together
inside the platform. (Individual-mode players: the notes feature below works
for you too — your notes are simply private to you.)

## Your team

Create a team when you join, or enter your team's **join code** to become a
member of an existing one. The team page shows your members and your solve
record. Team names appear on the scoreboard, so pick something you're happy
to see projected at the closing ceremony.

!!! tip "One solve is everyone's solve"
    Any member's correct flag scores for the whole team, and a challenge one
    of you solved shows as solved for all of you. Split the categories and
    divide the board — there's no reason to double-work a challenge.

## Shared challenge notes

Every challenge has a **notes scratchpad** shared with your team — a live
collaborative editor, private to your team, separate per challenge:

- Everyone can type at once; edits merge in real time, and you can see who
  else has the challenge open via the presence indicator.
- Notes persist for the whole event — capture partial progress, working
  theories, payloads that *almost* worked, and where you left off.
- In individual mode the same pad is personal: it syncs across your own
  devices instead.

Use it as the team's memory. The strongest CTF teams write down everything —
when someone rotates onto a challenge three hours in, the pad tells them
everything that's been tried.

## Coordinating in practice

A workflow that serves teams well:

1. **Claim loudly** — note "I'm on this" at the top of a challenge's pad so
   two people don't burn the same hour unknowingly.
2. **Dump progress as you go** — commands run, endpoints found, credentials
   discovered, dead ends hit.
3. **Rotate stuck challenges** — fresh eyes after a break beat tired eyes
   after four hours. The pad makes hand-offs cheap.
4. **Watch the value** — on dynamic scoring, a challenge your whole team is
   stuck on may be worth less by the hour; sometimes the winning move is to
    bank easier points first.
