## Your dashboard

**Dashboard** is where you land after signing in: **Your standing** (rank,
points, solved), **Your solves**, and the **Announcements** archive, newest
first — glance at it after any break.

## Announcements & notifications

Organisers broadcast **announcements** for anything event-wide. They arrive
live as a banner: a routine *Announcement* shows briefly then tucks into the
bell, an *Important* one stands out, and an *Urgent* one stays until you
dismiss it. Judges' **clarifications** arrive the same way.

The **bell** collects what's addressed to you — ticket replies,
announcements and clarifications, certificate releases, organiser alerts —
with an unread badge and **Mark all read**. Profile → **Notifications**
controls which categories come through (urgent announcements always do),
plus **Browser notifications** and a **Sound for support tickets**.

## Support tickets

When something's wrong — a target seems down, a sure flag is rejected,
you're out of guesses, or you have a rules question — open a ticket from the
**Support** page.

![Support](assets/support.png)
*New ticket: a subject, the related challenge, your message and optional
screenshots. Each ticket is a live conversation — replies appear in real
time, and you're notified when the judges answer.*

Pick the **Related challenge** rather than naming it in the text, say
exactly what you tried, attach **Screenshots** if they help, and keep one
issue per ticket. *A judge is looking at this ticket* means someone has it
open right now.

!!! tip "Ask early"
    Judges would far rather field a quick "is this challenge behaving?" than
    have you lose an hour to a broken target. You may be the first to notice.

## Pages, surveys and the assistant

Organisers often publish extra **pages** — full rules, prizes, venue or
sponsor info — listed at the bottom of the sidebar. If **Feedback** appears
there, a survey is open for you to **Respond** to. And if the organisers
have enabled the **Assistant**, it answers questions about the platform,
scoring and your standing; it never reveals flags, how much challenge help
it gives is up to the organisers, and staff can review the conversations —
it tells you so before you start.

## Conduct

The rules you accepted are the contract. The universal baseline: attack only
the challenge infrastructure you're pointed at — never the platform or other
competitors; don't share flags or solutions outside your team while the
event runs (instanced challenges can detect a borrowed flag); report
anything that looks like an unintended shortcut. Organisers can see
submission patterns — play it straight.

Good luck, and enjoy the event. 🚩
