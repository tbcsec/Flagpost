Everything about the competition itself lives in **Settings**, organised in
tabs.

![Competition settings](assets/settings.png)
*General holds identity and visibility; Controls holds the schedule and the
levers you'll pull mid-event; the remaining tabs hold challenge vocabulary,
rules, and optional feature panels.*

## Before the event

- **General** — name, description, individual vs team mode, visibility, and
  **brackets/divisions** (parallel rankings competitors self-select, e.g.
  Students and Open). A **public scoreboard** checkbox lists the event on
  the site's spectator page; a **CTFtime feed** checkbox exposes the
  standings format ctftime.org rates events from.
- **Rules** — the rules & code of conduct competitors must accept before
  playing. Change them mid-event and everyone is re-prompted before they can
  continue.
- **Modules** — per-competition toggles for optional features
  (automations, feedback, analytics, certificates, reports, AI assistant).

## The status gate

A competition is **not started → running → ended**. Competitors can only
open challenges and submit while it's *running* — before and after they see
a closed notice, while staff retain full access. The schedule can drive the
transitions automatically, and **Start/Stop** in Controls override it
manually at any moment.

## Mid-event levers (Controls)

- **Pause** — freezes submissions during an incident, with a banner for
  everyone. Staff can still submit, so you can verify the fix before
  resuming.
- **Scoreboard freeze** — stop public standings updates near the finale.
  Solves keep counting; reveal the final board when you're ready.
- **Guess caps & wrong-answer penalty** — competition-wide limits for
  multiple-choice challenges, and an optional percentage each wrong guess
  shaves off that question's value for that team.

!!! tip "Dry-run before doors open"
    While the competition is not started, play your own board: open every
    challenge, download every attachment, submit every flag. Ten minutes of
    staff dry-run catches most day-one tickets.
