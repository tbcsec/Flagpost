# Load test — 200-user baseline (2026-08-10)

- **Commit:** `98a517b` (pre-v1.4.0)
- **Scenario:** 200 concurrent users · 30 challenges · 800 held-open WebSockets · ~9.5 min
- **Stack:** production `docker compose` (Caddy → single uvicorn worker → Postgres/Redis/MinIO), fresh DB
- **Backend limit:** 4 vCPU / 4 GB (a realistic mid-size event VPS)
- **Harness:** [`sim.py`](sim.py) · raw metrics: [`2026-08-10-baseline-result.json`](2026-08-10-baseline-result.json)

## Verdict

**Holds at the door; buckles under sustained play — and it's concurrency-bound,
not CPU-bound.** The startup storm (200 logins + joins + 800 socket handshakes)
was a non-event. The failure is the sustained refetch storm every solve
triggers, colliding with a 15-connection database pool, compounded by
request-blocking fan-out (one announcement to all participants took **24
seconds**). Throughout the collapse the backend used under half its CPU budget,
so adding cores would not help. This is architectural and fixable without
abandoning the single-process design (ADR-0005).

## Bottlenecks found (→ v1.4.0 work)

Ordered by impact. Each links the tracking issue; every claim is grounded in
code (`file:line` in the issue).

1. **Activity-room refetch storm × tiny DB pool** — [#175](https://github.com/tbcsec/Flagpost/issues/175) + [#174](https://github.com/tbcsec/Flagpost/issues/174).
   One solve pings all clients; each refetches `["challenges"]`; hundreds of GETs
   queue behind 15 connections. *This is the first cliff.*
2. **Foreground fan-out blocks the event loop** — [#176](https://github.com/tbcsec/Flagpost/issues/176).
   Announcement/scoreboard broadcasts are awaited inside the mutation (the 24 s
   announcement). Move them to the existing background lane (ADR-0012).
3. **Sequential broadcast, no per-send timeout** — [#177](https://github.com/tbcsec/Flagpost/issues/177).
   One slow socket stalls the whole room (the 5.1 s stall).
4. **Unthrottled WS handshake + snapshot-per-join** — [#178](https://github.com/tbcsec/Flagpost/issues/178).
   A reconnect herd recomputes a scoreboard per socket.
5. **Scoreboard recompute + full-board egress** — [#87](https://github.com/tbcsec/Flagpost/issues/87).
   Real, but *not* the first cliff — reframed after this test (its original
   "fine to ~1,000 concurrent" premise was wrong; it showed seams at 200).

## The one chart that explains it

The DB pool rides its 15-connection cap in every burst while CPU sits well under
half its budget — the signature of a concurrency bottleneck, not a compute one.
(Raw series in the JSON; `pg_conns` vs `cpu` samples.)

```
DB pool util   ▁▁▂█▁▁█▁▂██▂▁  ~100% of cap, repeatedly
Backend CPU    ▂▁▂▄▁▂▄▁▂▄▂▁▁  ~10–42% of a 4-core budget
               └────────── steady play ──────────┘
```

## Caveats

- Absolute p95/p99 latencies are inflated by client-side queueing once the
  harness's own pool saturated — treat them as "congestion collapse," not exact
  SLAs. The server-side signals (pool pinned at 15, 24 s announce, idle CPU) are
  ground truth and independent of that.
- 200 real browsers open ~6 connections/origin each — *more* concurrency than
  the harness generated, so real load would be at least this bad.
- Single host, loopback, one IP, isolated Postgres — see [`README.md`](README.md)
  § "Honest limits".
